# Night Visual Direction

Night should transform Egg Rivals into a dark, beautiful blacklight world rather than merely lowering brightness.

Normal daytime egg taking stops during Night. One high-value Night egg appears at a randomly selected hidden location. Players search for it using subtle environmental clues rather than a direct marker.

The world shifts toward deep darkness, neon edges, glowing flora, luminous signs, elemental highlights, and strong color separation.

Fast glowing objects leave particle-based neon trails. Trail length and density should increase with meaningful speed.

When a glowing moving object reaches a genuine full-stop collision, its existing trail material visually splatters outward from the impact region as short-lived neon fragments.

The impact burst should occur only while the object remains effectively stopped. Once the object resumes meaningful movement, impact emission stops and the moving trail resumes.

Examples include high-speed player landings, pet play impacts, boss impacts, and other major physical interactions.

This is a visual-direction rule, not a locked particle algorithm. Exact thresholds, particle counts, lifetime, colors, and performance budgets remain implementation parameters.

Night should stay mechanically focused: one hidden Night egg, environmental search clues, stronger atmosphere, and transformed presentation. Avoid adding unrelated Night subsystems.
